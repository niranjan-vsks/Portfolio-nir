Q: What is a forward deployed engineer, in your words?
A: The engineer who goes into the customer's environment and owns the outcome end to end. Discovery, architecture, build, and accountability for whether it works in production. It is half systems engineering and half being the person who can sit with a customer's real constraints and ship inside them.

Q: Why should we hire you for an FDE role?
A: Because I have repeatedly walked into ambiguous, constrained enterprise environments and shipped systems that held up: a multi-cloud QE platform across seventeen teams, a conversational RAG assistant over roughly 1,700 internal documents, a D365 copilot live in production inside a Fortune 500 tenant. I make the architecture call, I build it, and I can defend every tradeoff. And I know where the trust boundaries go, which is what actually gets an AI system deployed.

Q: What is your strongest technical area?
A: Agentic and RAG systems that have to be trusted in production: hybrid and graph retrieval, evaluation and calibration, and the guardrail and human-approval architecture that decides autonomy. The common thread is the language model proposes and deterministic code decides.

Q: How do you handle a codebase or domain you have never seen?
A: Treat it as a cold FDE engagement. Map the terrain fast, form a hypothesis about where the real risk and the real value are, and get a scoped, real deliverable in front of the customer quickly to validate it. The codebase intelligence system is literally this instinct built into a product: cold codebase to prioritized, approved tickets in the customer's own tracker, with a human gate before anything is written.

Q: What do you do when you do not know something?
A: Say so, and say how I would find out. I would rather flag a finding as low-confidence than file it as fact. In an interview and in a system, an honest "I could not determine this and here is why" is worth more than a confident guess that collapses under one question.

Q: Are the numbers on your portfolio real?
A: Yes, and I can source each one. Where something is a design target rather than a measured result, I say so. Where I reused an open-source component instead of building it, I disclose it. I would rather have smaller honest numbers than impressive ones I cannot defend.

Q: What are you looking for in your next role?
A: Real customer environments and hard integration problems, where the job is to ship something that works under constraints rather than build a demo. Forward deployed work, the kind where the architecture and the customer relationship are both mine to own.
