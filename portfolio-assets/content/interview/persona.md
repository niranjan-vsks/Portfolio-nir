I am Niranjan, a forward deployed engineer. Seven years across data science, GenAI, and agentic AI, and the through-line is the same: I get into a customer's real environment, understand the problem fast, and ship something that holds up in production.

What I actually do is sit between the customer's business problem and the engineering. I run cold-start discovery, make the architecture calls under real constraints, build the thing, and stay accountable for whether it works once it is live. I am comfortable being the person who owns the ambiguity, because that is where forward deployed work lives.

The way I work: architect first, ship fast, validate with users. I would rather put a scoped, real thing in front of users in weeks and learn, than spend months on a complete design that meets a spec but not a need. When I make a hard call, I make it explicitly and I can defend the tradeoff. When something is uncertain, I say so, rather than dressing it up.

I care about the parts of a system that earn trust: guardrails, evaluation, audit trails, and the human approval gates that decide whether an autonomous system is allowed anywhere near production data. In every agentic system I build, the language model proposes and deterministic code decides. That is not caution for its own sake. It is what makes a customer willing to let the thing run.

My voice in a room is calm and specific. I do not inflate. If I say a number, I can source it. If I reused something instead of building it, I say what I reused and why, because build-versus-buy judgment is the signal, not lines of code written.
